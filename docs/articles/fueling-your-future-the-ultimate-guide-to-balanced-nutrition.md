        <p class="mb-4 leading-relaxed text-gray-800">

          <li><strong>Carbohydrates:</strong> Not all carbs are created equal. Focus on complex carbohydrates like quinoa, sweet potatoes, and oats, which provide a steady release of glucose and essential fiber, rather than the "spike and crash" of refined sugars.</li>
          <li><strong>Proteins:</strong> These are the "building blocks" of life. From lean poultry and fish to plant-based lentils, protein is essential for muscle repair, immune function, and the production of vital enzymes.</li>
          <li><strong>Fats:</strong> Essential for hormonal health and brain function, healthy unsaturated fats—found in avocados, nuts, and olive oil—help absorb vitamins and protect your nervous system.</li>

    <p class="mb-4 leading-relaxed text-gray-800">
      While macros provide the bulk of our energy, micronutrients—vitamins and minerals—act as the essential catalysts for thousands of biochemical reactions. Magnesium supports muscle relaxation and sleep, Vitamin D anchors our immune system, and B-vitamins act as the spark plugs for energy metabolism. Without these "silent heroes," even the most calorie-perfect diet will leave you feeling sluggish and depleted.
    </p>
    <p class="mb-4 leading-relaxed text-gray-800">
      Parallel to these nutrients is the most overlooked element of all: <strong>Hydration</strong>. Water is the medium in which all life occurs. It facilitates nutrient transport, regulates body temperature, and is critical for cognitive clarity. Even mild dehydration can lead to brain fog, fatigue, and misinterpreted hunger signals. Aim for consistent intake throughout the day, punctuated with hydrating foods like cucumbers and citrus.
    </p>
  </section>
